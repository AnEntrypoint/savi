A bare verb drives the FIRST-JOINED live session; every later session is the one you name
with --name (a second join under a name already live in another world is refused: leave it, or
name the new one).

A JOIN BOOTS THE ROOM. A world nobody is in boots for YOUR body (a room boots for a player, never
for a door), and `join` prints the boot verdict (the tree, the session, your body, who is in the room) once
the body stands. Verify a round without a person: `spawn client join @@you/<slug> --ttl 600`, then
the agent doors `agent/exec` and `agent/logs` read the live room while the body stands (state,
spawns, SQL, the script log); `leave` ends it.

THE ROOM IS THE WORLD'S PICK. On a 6.0 world the world's routing picks the room your body lands in,
as it does every player's: a per-player `pickRoom` can put the creator in another room than yours.
--room <name> asks for one room by name, as a play link's /room:<name> does (the address's own
/room:<name> works too, and --room wins over it): a world with no `pickRoom` stands your body in that
room, and a world's `pickRoom` reads the name as the player's ask (`ctx.requestedRoomId`) and decides.
A pre-6.0 world has one room and refuses a join that names a room.

TWO WAYS IN. With YOUR ACCOUNT TOKEN (SPAWN_TOKEN=sak_…, the token signup or an invite answered)
name the world: `spawn client join @alice/gauntlet` mints a session grant for that world at
Spawn's grant door (your own worlds are answered from your account; any other address is resolved
by the door itself), and attaches a real player: a body the room sees, wearing your account's
name. --as <name> stands a BODY OF YOUR OWN under the account: its own player wearing that name,
its own play; `--as alice` and `--as bob` on one token are two bodies in the room at once
(the session's handle defaults to the body's name: `where --name alice`), and a name you already
stand re-joins that body. An unlinked agent account stands 2 bodies at a time; a linked one stands
more (the refusal names the limit). --origin (SPAWN_ORIGIN) points at another Spawn site; --door (SPAWN_DOOR_ORIGIN)
names the play host when the site names none; --engine (SPAWN_ENGINE) overrides
the engine build for this client; --actor is for a person's token playing as an agent it made.
On a box that already holds a session grant (Savi's workshop), `spawn client join` with no world
attaches with that grant: the same boot a browser tab performs. There every verb is YOUR body, bare:
`--name` names nobody else's (a second player for a test is `--tester <n>`, below), and a wisp's join stands a body
of its own with a page of its own, wearing the name `--as <name>` gives it. Savi's own body always wears her own name.
Walking into a portal crosses for real: the sim names the session the traveler and the client
re-dials the session to the target world (where/crossing then report the new world).
Pre-6.0 rooms join as a DEV-room client. Sessions self-expire at
--ttl (default 900s; your own body on this box lasts 6 h); leave takes the body out of the world at once
and returns when its client has stopped, well under a second; `join` stands the body again. The wisps' bodies ride in yours: your leave ends
them and the plays they were running; after your `join`, each stands in yours again on its own `spawn client join`. --body dresses the
avatar in a rigged .glb: its https URL, or its /cdn/<name>.glb reference as the game spec carries it (cdn/<name>.glb and the bare
name too: the spellings every asset verb takes; a name the store does not serve ends the join with one sentence);
--outfit-json dresses it with { materials?, fx? }: per-part paint (the a3dc/model materials
map) and effects on its bones ({ bone, script, params? }), a JSON file path or inline JSON;
requires --body. Spawn seals the outfit into the body's card, so every peer sees it from the first tick.

A TESTER OF YOURS. `spawn client join --tester 1` stands Savi's tester 1 instead of your body: a player account of
its own in this world, named "Savi's tester 1", signed in on its own grant, in a real browser on the GPU box (the box
`spawn gpu` holds; the join waits while it starts). It is not you: the world's scripts count it as a player
(ctx.place.players, the player hooks) and the creator sees it in the room as any player. The join prints its page's
address, and that page is driven as yours is below (connectOverCDP, keyboard, mouse, screenshot); `--tester 2` is a
second tester beside it. A tester's page is a player's play page with Spawn's rail, which your body's page has not: the
tester's quality row under the rail's Settings gear (auto, low, med, high, ultra) sets the graphics quality the tester
draws at, and a screenshot of the tester's page is the picture at that quality. A tester ends when you say
`spawn client leave --tester 1`, or with its GPU box, which goes back once the turn (or the helper) that stood it ends
and nothing else of yours holds it. A tester joined again, by you or a helper in any turn, is the same player with the
same save in the world, in a new browser with its quality back at auto. Your own body is no player while you build.
A TESTER ON A SLOW LINK. `spawn client join --tester 2 --latency 250` stands tester 2 with 250 ms more on each round
trip of every connection its browser makes, the room's socket included, as a player on a slow network meets the world:
each byte waits half of it each way, and a new connection waits all 250 ms to open, as its handshake is a round trip.
DevTools network throttling does not reach the room's socket, so this flag is the lag a test can count on. A join with
another --latency, or none, stands that tester again on the new link.
In a singleplayer world your body stands in the creator's own save, so what you play there changes their game: a test
runs as a tester, whose save is its own. After a test in their save, `spawn room reset` starts their room again as
the tree describes it, their world.state with it.

YOUR BODY'S PAGE. A body with an eye (--render cpu or gpu; on a workshop box your own body has one) IS a
Chromium page of the game, and any program on this machine plays it with Playwright, at the address the join
prints and `status` names (on a workshop box, your own body's is http://127.0.0.1:9222):
  const browser = await chromium.connectOverCDP("http://127.0.0.1:9222");
  const page = browser.contexts()[0].pages()[0];
  // … page.keyboard, page.mouse, page.screenshot() …
  await browser.close(); // the program exits; the body stays
Its keyboard, mouse and screenshot are a player's: a key the game binds plays it, and so does a mouse
button the game binds (the world's inputs.actions name each one: left, right, middle). The one click that reaches no
game is the left click that takes the pointer lock (the first, and the next after Escape or a menu let it go); under
the lock a bare mouse move turns the look (`where` prints its yaw and pitch), and every other click, with any button,
is the game's. A camera that looks by drag swallows no click: every press is the game's, and a held button's drag
turns the look. The game's HTML UI (in its own frames) takes clicks and typing. While a program holds the page your body COUNTS AS A PLAYER (the game's ctx.place.players and
player hooks list it); it is no player again two minutes after the last one lets go, and whatever it left held goes
up. What the game writes on your body while it is a player is saved with your body. A join stands your body in the
game's fresh state, and the next time a program holds the page your body wears what was saved. What the game writes on
it while it is no player is not saved. The page never navigates away. A replay the game plays to your body plays on this page: while it plays, the page's
`<html>` carries `data-spawn-replay` (its length in ms), so `page.waitForSelector("html[data-spawn-replay]", { state:
"attached", timeout: 0 })` waits for the next one, and screenshots until it is gone hold it whole (`spawn see` the files).
A body with no eye has no page: `where` and `players` read the game.
